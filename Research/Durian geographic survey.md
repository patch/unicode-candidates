This is an evidence-led geographic survey for the durian-symbol project. Its purpose is to establish where durians are biologically native, cultivated as a market crop, consumed beyond predominantly immigrant communities, imported into diaspora markets, and represented visually in functional and marketing contexts. It is background research that can inform arguments for or against encoding; it is not written to predetermine the conclusion.

The project definition of a **country** is any territory assigned an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code, including dependent territories. The `country_code` property is the primary identifier in the country records and in [[Durian countries.base]]. Territory names use Unicode CLDR English (`en`) forms, preferring a non-abbreviated `alt="short"` value when available, and may change without changing the record identity ([M03](Sources.md#M03)).

## Symbol referent

The biological findings do not define the prospective character as a species symbol. In popular usage, “durian” refers to the recognizable spiky fruit and edible-fruit category produced by several *Durio* species. *Durio zibethinus* is the strongest **popular representative species** because it dominates cultivation and trade, but the character should follow that broader dictionary-like real-world sense rather than operate as biological nomenclature. A character’s semantic scope need not be monophyletic if ordinary use is broader.

These sets answer different questions and should not be merged into a single “durian country” category. For example, Singapore is in the native genus range and is a major consumer but is not a significant current producer; Puerto Rico has niche commercial cultivation but no evidence here of population-wide consumption; and France is a meaningful import market whose observed consumption is still principally tied to Asian retail and diaspora contexts.

## How the original brief was refined

The user authorized a more formal framing where it improves future publication. Four material changes were made:

1. **Native range is taxon-specific.** The notes distinguish any accepted *Durio* species from *D. zibethinus*, the principal cultivated fruit. Calling every country with a wild *Durio* species “native for durian” would conceal this difference.
2. **Commercial cultivation is tiered.** “Commercial” ranges from a national industry with official production statistics to a few market farms. Records therefore distinguish industrial or statistically established production, niche commercial production, and an emerging commercial project. Plausible but weakly sourced cases are retained only as leads.
3. **Consumption is classified by social reach.** National or territory-wide consumption, substantial regional consumption, emerging general-market niches, and primarily diaspora-oriented markets are separate categories. Import value alone is not treated as proof of general-population consumption.
4. **Visual use is separated by function.** Non-marketing signage is maintained as a stronger, separate occurrence register. Marketing and brand use remains relevant, especially when independent organizations converge on a durian form or integrate it into ordinary text. An exhaustive worldwide negative cannot be established from web research.

Fruit rankings were produced only where a source-defined dataset actually includes durian. A production rank, import rank, preference survey, and apparent-consumption rank are labelled as different measures rather than made to look comparable.

## Inclusion rules

### Native

“Native” follows current botanical distribution sources, primarily Kew Plants of the World Online. Biogeographic units such as Borneo and Sumatra are mapped to present territories identified by ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes. An introduced population, even if long established, is not native.

### Commercially grown

A country qualifies as verified market-oriented cultivation when a government statistic, agricultural authority, scholarly source, or well-documented grower report shows fruit produced for sale or an explicitly commercial planting. The tiers are:

- **Industrial/statistically established:** official production statistics, a documented national or regional industry, or both.
- **Niche commercial:** sale from small plantings is documented, but no broad industry is demonstrated.
- **Emerging project:** an active planting or programme expressly intended for a commercial market, without demonstrated recurring production.
- **Candidate/unverified:** plausible cultivation evidence exists, but sale, scale, continuity, or source quality is insufficient.

### At-large consumption

Evidence must show consumption or demand among a national, territorial, or substantial regional public rather than only specialist shops serving immigrants. Suitable evidence includes consumption surveys, official food-availability data, widespread domestic retail, cultural reporting, or import scale that is persuasive in a small established durian market. “Regional” means a geographically concentrated part of the resident population, such as Davao/Mindanao or Zanzibar, not an ethnic diaspora alone.

### Global reach through diaspora import markets

The purpose is to document distribution and plausible repeated exposure beyond the core region, and to identify places where comprehension can later be tested. The screening rule is at least US$1 million of reported 2023 fresh-durian imports under HS 081060, followed by demographic and retail-context research. The United Kingdom and New Zealand are included below the threshold because their population geography makes them useful comparison cases. The Netherlands is a redistribution control and is not labelled diaspora-driven.

Trade statistics do not identify consumers’ ethnicity and do not demonstrate recognition of a durian symbol. A diaspora interpretation is therefore explicitly marked as direct evidence, supported inference, or a research hypothesis. The evidence ladder is distribution → repeated local exposure → observed local symbol use → tested comprehension.

### Signage

Priority is given to signs that regulate behaviour in transport, accommodation, buildings, or other shared spaces. Advertising, retail displays, and promotional festival signs belong to the separate marketing-evidence layer rather than being discarded. A photograph with a location and date is strongest; a scholarly sign corpus or a specific contemporary account can establish a documented occurrence. Airline baggage rules without a photographed physical sign are policy context only.

## Limits of the survey

- HS 081060 covers **fresh durians**. Frozen fruit, pulp, paste, confectionery, prepared foods, and some e-commerce flows are omitted.
- Re-exports can make a port or logistics centre look like a consumer market. Hong Kong and the Netherlands need particular care.
- Official crop statistics are not synchronized by year and may differ in whether young orchards, bearing area, or backyard trees are counted.
- “Durian” most often means fruit of *D. zibethinus* in commerce but has broader genus-level and vernacular senses. This affects biological claims but does not create a requirement to force character semantics into a species boundary.
- Web-visible signs are biased towards tourist hotels, major cities, and photographers who post on indexed platforms.
- Most sign photographs found are not cleared for proposal publication. Evidential usability and publication rights are separate.
- National fruit-consumption systems rarely publish a single complete league table including seasonal durian. Absence from a ranking is often a data-design issue, not evidence of low consumption.

## Overall interpretation

The durian has a compact biological centre in western Malesia and adjacent mainland Southeast Asia, a larger cultivation and at-large consumption zone across Southeast Asia and southern China, and a much wider import footprint. The strongest functional-symbol evidence is narrower but geographically repeated: prohibition signs are independently documented in six Southeast Asian territories and occur in transport, hotels, and general house-rule settings. Their communicative function is stable—the whole fruit or a durian pictogram marks an odour-related prohibition—even when the wording and venue differ.

Marketing evidence is broader but currently less systematically collected. A first search found a durian form replacing LATIN CAPITAL LETTER O (`U+004F`) in a university packaging-design project and a durian-like mark functioning as FULL STOP (`U+002E`) in a transnational commercial wordmark. Both can be treated as stylistic renditions of existing encoded characters, so they do not independently demonstrate an encoding gap. Their narrower value is evidence that two different brand identities reduce the durian to a simplified, character-scale form within type design.

China’s import demand dominates the monetary trade data, but it should not eclipse the older cultural and agricultural centre. Conversely, substantial imports into Europe, North America, Australia, and the Gulf do not by themselves establish that durian is consumed by the general public there. This separation is important for any later claim about geographic breadth.

## Recommended next research

1. **Catalogue the existing sign-photo collection.** When the project owner can share it, preserve originals, record provenance and rights, code venue, wording, visual form, and independence, and identify evidential and publication gaps before commissioning or broadly collecting more images.
2. **Verify the signage leads.** Seek traceable physical examples in BN, HK, LA, PH, and TW, then expand to other Southeast Asian countries without assuming occurrence.
3. **Document symbol form and marketing convergence.** Code each sign for whole-fruit silhouette, cut fruit, colour, spikes, stalk, slash, enclosing circle, accompanying `Language Name (language_code)`, and whether the fruit remains recognizable at small size. Search trademark databases, packaging archives, and brand portfolios for independent durian marks, including pictorial substitutions for letters such as “O” and punctuation-like uses.
4. **Compare prohibition-overlay precedents.** Build a bounded repertoire of Unicode characters known to appear on real signage with a circle, diagonal slash or cross overlay, including cigarettes, mobile phones and skateboards. Distinguish the encoded base character, overlay convention, venue and function, and evidence of public recognition.
5. **Obtain primary trade microdata.** Download UN Comtrade reporter/partner/quantity records for 2019–2025, add frozen/prepared durian codes where separable, and identify re-export hubs.
6. **Standardize production.** Create a year-by-year table from national statistical agencies with harvested area, output, and data definition. Confirm or reject BR, CR, MG, and any Pacific leads through agricultural ministries or census tables.
7. **Improve consumption comparisons.** Request unpublished commodity detail from national household-expenditure, total-diet, or supply-utilization programmes. Prefer annual mass per capita and record the denominator and food universe.
8. **Test global reach and comprehension.** Map durian retailers and distributor interviews against census geography in the listed local regions. Document packaging, local-language media, festivals, and signs; then test unaided and aided recognition among diaspora and non-diaspora residents. Ask who buys fresh versus frozen durian; do not infer purchaser identity from neighbourhood alone.
9. **Test plain-text need and counterarguments after the photo audit.** Assess whether documented functions require public interchange in plain text, whether existing text or characters suffice, whether visual use is merely illustrative, and which findings would change the working judgement.
10. **Build any additional rights-cleared sign corpus justified by the gap audit.** Commission or request missing photographs from relevant transit operators, hotels, libraries, hospitals, and building managers. Capture the whole sign in context, exact coordinates, date, photographer, and written permission.
11. **Preserve negative and uncertain findings.** Record ministry enquiries and searches that find no commercial crop or no sign. They will make future “all countries” claims auditable.
12. **Reuse and improve the method.** Apply [[Geographic evidence research for symbol proposals]] to the next subject under evaluation, append lessons to its refinement log, and review whether earlier data need reclassification.

## Review status

This survey is a strong first-pass background dataset, not a closed world census. Each country record carries a review date and evidence confidence. Re-run taxonomy, trade, and production checks before using figures in a formal publication.
