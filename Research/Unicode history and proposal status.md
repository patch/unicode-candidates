This note reconstructs the documented Unicode history of a durian character or emoji from original external sources. It distinguishes public discussion, formal Unicode documents, emoji submissions, committee responses, and current procedural consequences. It does not rely on the earlier private-vault note as a source.

## Current finding

Durian has been discussed publicly in Unicode channels since 2015, appeared in formal documents during the Unicode 11.0 review, and has three entries in Unicode’s emoji-proposal status record. None of those events amounts to approval. The most recent recorded emoji submission was later declined. The available public sources bracket the 2022 batch process, but do not establish an official re-review eligibility date under the current four-year wording.

The character-proposal and emoji-proposal paths remain distinct. The emoji restriction does not state that research or a character proposal to the Script Encoding Working Group must stop. Prepare the character case independently and treat emoji re-review as a step whose eligibility should be confirmed with the Emoji Standard & Research Working Group.

## Emoji status record

The [Unicode emoji-proposal status page](https://unicode.org/emoji/emoji-proposals-status.html) embeds a public spreadsheet with the columns ‘Emoji’, ‘Status’, and ‘Date Submitted’. Its three durian rows read as follows when accessed on 22 July 2026:

| Emoji | Status | Date submitted |
|---|---|---:|
| Durian (A) | Expired | 21 September 2017 |
| Durian (B) | Declined | 20 November 2018 |
| durian | Declined | 24 July 2022 |

Unicode defines ‘Expired’ as a proposal returned for modification and not returned within 60 days, usually because of deficiencies in the initial proposal. It defines ‘Declined’ as not fulfilling the selection criteria and says declined emoji cannot be resubmitted for four years. These generic definitions do not disclose the exact history or reasoning for an individual row.

The page says that the list is normally updated once a year, usually in early December, and omits automatically declined proposals. It should therefore be treated as the current published summary rather than a complete event log. The archived sheet captured on 2 December 2022 confirms that the durian outcome was public by then; it does not prove when the row was first published. ([U12](Sources.md#U12))

## Present procedural consequence

The public material supports a date bracket, not one settled eligibility date. It also shows that the waiting period changed: archived guidance used two years in November 2022 and November 2023, while the checked November 2024 snapshot and current guidance use four years. The wording checked does not say whether the four-year period applies retroactively to a 2022 decline or whether the period starts at submission, committee decision, author notification, or public status publication. ([U06](Sources.md#U06), [U13](Sources.md#U13)) A strict decision-date calculation is therefore one possible administrative reading, not a conclusion compelled by the published statements.

The following dates are useful for planning while preserving that uncertainty:

| Date | Basis | Interpretation and limit |
|---|---|---|
| 24 July 2026 | Four years after the status sheet’s ‘Date Submitted’ | Earliest plausible anniversary. No checked guidance says that submission starts the clock. |
| Shortly after 3 November 2026 | Four years after the end of the fourth-quarter meeting, after which the 2022 authors were to be notified | Probable batch-notification anniversary, but no exact notification or durian decision date is public. |
| 3 December 2026 | More than four years after the archived sheet captured on 2 December 2022 already marked durian ‘Declined’ | Conservative planning backstop if the present rule is applied retroactively and the relevant event occurred no later than public recording. It is not an official determination. |

The project should ask the Emoji Standard & Research Working Group whether a durian re-review is eligible, which event it treats as controlling, and whether the four-year rule applies to the 2022 decision. Until then, do not present any of the dates above as Unicode’s confirmed eligibility date.

The current emoji guidelines separately direct a widely used symbol that does not require colour towards the character-proposal process. The Script Encoding Working Group accepts character proposals and explicitly does not accept emoji proposals. Its current criteria require existing community use, a stable character repertoire, and a need for public interchange in plain text. ([U06](Sources.md#U06), [U07](Sources.md#U07)) The present emoji re-review restriction is not stated as a bar to that character route, although the character case must succeed on its own criteria.

## What the history does not establish

- The 2015 mailing-list exchange does not establish that a formal proposal was filed.
- L2/17-393 records expert comments, not a decision to encode or recommend durian.
- L2/17-400 does not identify which of its three grouped explanations applied to durian.
- L2/18-027 welcomes a proposal but gives no assessment that it would meet the selection criteria.
- The status spreadsheet does not show proposer names, submitted documents, decline dates, or item-specific reasons.
- The checked public guidance does not define the start of the waiting period, explain the change from two years to four, or state its transitional application.
- The archived 2 December 2022 sheet establishes only that the ‘Declined’ status was public no later than that capture.
- No itemized public batch announcement naming the declined 2022 submissions was located in the official sources checked. The batch reports and the archived status sheet provide different parts of the date bracket.
- The sources checked do not establish that ‘Durian (A)’, ‘Durian (B)’, or ‘durian’ is the submission discussed in any particular L2 document. Chronological proximity is not proof of identity.
- A declined emoji proposal does not decide whether the real-world sign is a distinct character suitable for plain-text interchange. That is a separate claim requiring separate evidence.

## Historical link checks

- The obsolete `http://unicode.org/emoji/selection.html` URL cited by L2/18-027 now serves a meta-refresh to the [current emoji-proposal guidelines](https://unicode.org/emoji/proposals.html). A [16 January 2018 Internet Archive capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20180116121410/http://www.unicode.org:80/emoji/selection.html) preserves the period page and its then-current proposal form and selection guidance. ([U15](Sources.md#U15))
- The exact Google search for `durian emoji “no hugs”` cited in L2/17-393 was visited on 22 July 2026. It returned a Google troubleshooting shell rather than a stable, reproducible result set and should not be used as evidence.
- The 2015 mailing-list discussion links to a CafePress durian-sticker results page as the source of the ‘no hugs’ example. The live URL returned HTTP 403 on 22 July 2026, but a [22 August 2015 Internet Archive capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20150822000743/http://www.cafepress.co.uk:80/+durian+stickers) preserves the listing ‘Sad Durian that gets no hugs Decal’ and associated product metadata. ([U14](Sources.md#U14)) This verifies the historical reference, but it remains third-party marketing art with unverified reuse rights rather than evidence of functional symbol use.

## Research leads

- Ask the Emoji Standard & Research Working Group whether a durian re-review is eligible, which date or event starts the waiting period, and how the four-year rule applies to a decision made while the published rule was two years.
- Seek public or author-held copies of the three submitted proposals and any response correspondence. Record provenance and rights before storing them.
- If proposal documents are recovered, compare their arguments and evidence with the present character case rather than assuming that the earlier declines addressed signage or plain-text interchange.
