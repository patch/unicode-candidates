This note records the current project decisions and intentions needed by collaborators working at different times. It is not a research log, a proposal draft, or a fixed plan for the vault’s structure.

## Current objective

Research whether a durian character has a sound case for encoding, including evidence and reasoning both for and against it, and prepare a proposal if the evidence continues to support that conclusion. My present judgement favours encoding, particularly because of durian’s repeated use on signage in Southeast Asia, but the research remains open to disconfirming evidence and better interpretations. The signage establishes a prima facie visual convention; it does not yet establish need for public interchange in plain text.

## Submission strategy

Develop the Character and Emoji cases as active parallel research tracks. Re-review eligibility gates only an Emoji submission, not the priority or scope of Emoji research. The eligibility timing remains unresolved under the public record; the evidence and possible cycle-based and elapsed-time interpretations are documented at [Unicode history and proposal status](<Research/Unicode history and proposal status.md>).

Current Unicode guidance does not accept proposals to emojify existing characters. The project must therefore not treat a character-first encoding as a stepping stone to later emoji treatment. If standardized emoji treatment remains a material objective and the Emoji case is credible, prefer an Emoji-first submission or obtain route-specific guidance before a non-emoji encoding could foreclose that option. The Character case remains independently useful and must satisfy its own criteria; the two research tracks should share evidence without implying that either route guarantees the other outcome. See the shared [Emoji and character proposal routing](<../../Research/Emoji and character proposal routing.md>) analysis.

## Research status

I have already conducted years of research, but not all of it has been contributed or reviewed. Contribution and source review are in progress, and the prior Unicode history has been reconstructed from its external sources at [Unicode history and proposal status](<Research/Unicode history and proposal status.md>).

The candidate-specific [evidence-to-encoding-question map](<Research/Evidence to encoding-question map.md>) applies the shared route and analytical labels from the project’s [character-identity and encoding method](<../../Methods/Character identity and encoding analysis.md>). It separates Character criteria from Emoji selection factors, project analytical questions and the candidate-specific counterargument, while distinguishing decision-bearing evidence from contextual material that may be well sourced but has little relevance to one or both routes. It currently supports active parallel Character and Emoji research and deferral rather than proposal drafting. Functional signage remains the strongest Character-route support, while the bounded [plain-text-interchange and counterargument audit](<Research/Plain-text interchange and counterargument audit.md>) found no documented present demand, workaround or concrete loss. That absence does not count against prospective use because no standardized durian character exists. The Emoji route still needs a bounded factor and query-plan pass.

The candidate-specific [proposal requirements ledger](<Research/Proposal requirements ledger.md>) maps that evidence to the current official submission fields and the shared project decisions needed to answer them. Its initial application confirms the same deferral while exposing the administrative, evidential, technical, rights and packaging fields that are already supportable, provisional, inapplicable or blocked; completing it is not itself a decision to submit or a change to the candidate phase.

The [contributor photo corpus synthesis](<Research/Contributor photo corpus synthesis.md>) consolidates 165 privately held images into 91 events and deep-reviews seven functional groups plus three adverse, comparative or mixed groups. The seven functional groups contain 11 rule or handling-instruction image rows and seven contextual companions. They reduce to five observed sign-design lineages because two Sentosa deployments share one operator design and two retail deployments share one illustrated template. This strengthens the evidence that a generic durian referent persists across varied sign artwork, but it does not establish unaided recognition, independent invention, an abstract character, or a plain-text workflow.

The evidence-led [Durian geographic survey](<Research/Durian geographic survey.md>) is a first-pass dataset with explicit confidence tiers and open research leads, not a closed worldwide census. Its reusable lessons are maintained separately in [Geographic evidence research for symbol proposals](<../../Methods/Geographic evidence research for symbol proposals.md>) so later symbol projects can repeat and refine the process without importing durian-specific conclusions.

The bounded review at [Existing Unicode characters with fruit referents](<../../Research/Existing Unicode characters with fruit referents.md>) covers fruit-referent characters in Unicode 17.0.0. It leads with the contemporary emoji comparison, separates weak legacy and historical material, distinguishes encoded characters from emoji presentation and sequences, and preserves botanical and culinary disagreement. The reusable repertoire-audit method is at [Comparative Unicode repertoire research](<../../Methods/Comparative Unicode repertoire research.md>).

That review also establishes the project’s treatment of representational diversity. An encoded fruit identity and its representative emoji design do not enumerate every familiar depiction of the fruit. The five observed Durian designs are therefore not adverse merely because they include whole, paired, cut-open, filled and outline forms. Where the referent persists, the diversity supports independence from one artwork; it becomes adverse only if a difference carries a separate identity, recognition fails, or the meaning depends on exact contextual presentation. Selecting a proposal glyph remains necessary for drafting but is not the same question as proving that one depiction dominated before encoding.

## Strongest current non-encoding model

The strongest credible alternative is that the observed durian graphics are ordinary pictograms or illustrations embedded in physical signs, not uses of an abstract character in textual interchange. On this model, a venue commissions or selects a recognizable fruit image, combines it with a prohibition circle or slash, words, colour and layout, and communicates through the resulting sign as a visual artefact. Repetition of the referent and regulatory function can establish an icon convention without establishing that operators, designers or readers preserve a durian unit when the artwork and layout are removed.

The presently documented workflows use available representations. Ordinary text names durian and states the rule across transit policies, room rules, web pages, booking terms and PDFs; an image or icon asset preserves exact pictogram artwork; and metadata, markup and higher-level symbol systems can identify and manage graphical content. Existing characters also represent the letter and punctuation functions in the documented inline brand uses. These observations define the current alternatives, but do not establish that users would prefer them for every operation if a durian character existed. An encoded character would not preserve exact artwork or the complete rule; it could instead supply an artwork-independent semantic unit alongside those representations.

## Evidence presently supporting or weakening that model

- **Functional signage and interchange:** [Durian signage](<Research/Durian signage.md>) verifies prohibition use in six Southeast Asian territories across transit, accommodation and building rules. The [contributor corpus audit](<Research/Contributor photo corpus synthesis.md>) additionally finds five visibly distinct functional sign designs across seven photographed event groups, with whole, paired, cut-open, filled and outline durian forms. Two pairs are repeat deployments of the same artwork rather than independent designs. The variation is positive evidence against a one-artwork identity and is consistent with one generic referent across depictions; it is not an encoding objection merely because the forms differ. The [interchange audit](<Research/Plain-text interchange and counterargument audit.md>) documents how transit, accommodation and retail rules are represented today, but found no stated demand, workaround or concrete loss. Direct character interchange cannot be expected before encoding, so this remains a gap in affirmative need evidence rather than adverse evidence about future use.
- **Adverse form evidence:** The contributor corpus also contains an ambiguous painted fruit, a pomelo package using a durian-like spiky whole-fruit image, and a soursop product named ‘durian belanda’. These findings show that spikes, stalk, yellow flesh-like areas, or the lexical string ‘durian’ are not individually sufficient to identify the candidate referent. Wording and context remain materially involved in the current recognition evidence.
- **Marketing and inline integration:** [Durian visual use](<Research/Durian visual use.md>) records character-scale forms, but the clearest examples are directly explainable as a stylized U+004F O <span style="font-variant:all-small-caps">LATIN CAPITAL LETTER O</span>, U+002E . <span style="font-variant:all-small-caps">FULL STOP</span> or a letter–image hybrid. This positively supports the logo or glyph-treatment branch of the non-encoding model for those occurrences; it does not decide the different functional-signage evidence. One example is a design project rather than verified sustained deployment.
- **Repertoire comparison:** the [fruit-referent review](<../../Research/Existing Unicode characters with fruit referents.md>) finds a large contemporary emoji neighbourhood and no durian representation in its bounded Unicode 17.0.0 corpus. This weakens the claim that an existing fruit character already supplies the same referent, but repertoire fit, category completion and visual recognizability do not establish plain-text need. Legacy terminal symbols and historical commodity signs arose from materially different encoding requirements.
- **Subject importance and geographic reach:** the [geographic survey](<Research/Durian geographic survey.md>) establishes a substantial biological, commercial and cultural footprint. For the Character route, this makes the referent and geographic context intelligible, but the material remains background rather than evidence that the pictogram is an abstract textual unit. For active Emoji research, the same material may help select relevant languages, regions and historically grounded search terms for Usage level and Frequency evidence, and cultural persistence may inform the Transient factor. It is not itself prescribed Frequency evidence or proof that any Emoji factor is met.
- **Prior Unicode treatment:** the [Unicode history](<Research/Unicode history and proposal status.md>) supplies no item-specific reason for the 2022 emoji decline, while the earlier records concern an expired item and a duplicate. Those outcomes are therefore neither three adverse merits findings nor favourable character-route evidence. The missing reasons are an evidential limit, not evidence for the non-encoding model.

## Decision-changing evidence

| Possible finding | Likely effect on the working decision |
|---|---|
| Independent sign systems use varied durian forms as the same named semantic unit, and users must enter, exchange, quote, search, index or preserve that unit outside the source artwork; words, images, markup or existing representations cause a documented loss in those workflows | Materially strengthen support for encoding |
| Relevant users or systems prefer current alternatives for the same prospective operations, do not treat the durian referent as an artwork-independent unit or would gain no material function from encoding | Materially strengthen opposition to encoding; the public-source audit did not establish this |
| No present demand, workaround or concrete loss is found, while future uptake cannot be observed because no standardized character exists | Preserve the interchange question as an affirmative evidence gap and support deferral; do not count the absence as evidence against future use |
| A conventionally used existing character or standardized sequence is found to carry the same identity and behave adequately in the relevant workflows | Materially weaken a separate-character case and redirect it towards the existing representation |
| A stable generic referent is established across varied forms, but its plain-text users, operations and losses remain unproved | The form diversity supports identity independent of one artwork; the interchange gap supports Character-route deferral. An unselected representative glyph remains a drafting dependency, not an adverse eligibility finding |
| Evidence supports a generic durian-fruit unit across renderings, while the prohibition circle, slash, wording and brand treatments remain ordinary composition | Narrow the model to a generic durian character; exclude a separately encoded “no durian” sign, exact artwork, logos and species-level semantics |

## Current dependencies and unresolved questions

The private sign-photo collection has been catalogued and audited at event level, and the public-source phase of the plain-text-interchange audit is complete. Private originals, precise coordinates, comments and mappings remain outside the public repository. These audits make broad new sign and policy collection lower priority until a named evidential or publication gap justifies it.

The next decision-bearing Character evidence requires human consultation and therefore project-owner authority. Sign-system designers, operators or wayfinding specialists should establish whether organizations treat the recurring durian pictogram as a reusable semantic token independent of its artwork, or simply select an illustrative asset for each displayed sign. Relevant users and text-system implementers must identify current workarounds and whether they would use an encoded unit for plain-text input, composition, interchange, fallback, search, indexing, quotation or archival retrieval, then compare its prospective benefit with words, existing characters or sequences, images, metadata and higher-level protocols. The exact bounded questions are preserved in the [interchange audit](<Research/Plain-text interchange and counterargument audit.md#decision-and-project-consequence>).

Until that consultation is authorized, the highest-priority actionable central task is the bounded Emoji factor and query-plan pass. The five observed functional designs also justify a later, separate unlabelled recognition and small monochrome test, but the adverse comparators show why spikes or a stalk alone cannot define the answer in advance. Until user conception, plain-text operations, concrete losses, design-source independence and a suitable representative glyph are tested, the project cannot settle character identity or proposal-stage properties.

## 2026 emoji-submission timing and eligibility

The Unicode Consortium’s [emoji-proposal guidelines](https://www.unicode.org/emoji/proposals.html), last updated 20 May 2026, state that the current submission window closes at the end of 31 July 2026. They also state: ‘Emoji declined within the last four years are not eligible for re-review.’ Both points were rechecked on 22 July 2026.

The latest durian entry was submitted on 24 July 2022. Official committee records say that the 2022 review was complete by 31 October, that authors would be notified shortly after the 1–3 November Unicode Technical Committee meeting, and an Internet Archive capture shows the durian row publicly marked ‘Declined’ by 2 December 2022. The published status record does not give an item-specific decision or notification date.

The historical rule also matters: archived guidance still said two years in November 2022 and November 2023, but said four years by November 2024. The public wording does not say whether the later rule applies retroactively, whether ‘four years’ refers to four annual submission cycles, or whether an elapsed period runs from submission, decision, notification or publication. Under a cycle-based reading, a proposal declined in the 2022 cycle could be eligible in the 2026 cycle; under elapsed-time readings, the plausible planning dates run from 24 July through early December 2026. None is an official eligibility determination, and the 2026 cycle must not be characterized as either available or unavailable without confirmation from the Emoji Standard & Research Working Group. This uncertainty affects submission, not Emoji research priority. See [Unicode history and proposal status](<Research/Unicode history and proposal status.md>).

The project will accumulate a larger archive of images than will appear in the formal proposal. Only a select set of the strongest representative images is intended for the proposal. A larger, but still curated, subset may be used for a general-audience project page at `novapatch.ie/en/durian`.

Research from this and other character-proposal projects may become source material for corresponding pages on my personal website, whether or not a proposal is approved.

Original project text and assets are intended for publication under CC BY-SA 4.0. Third-party evidence remains excluded from that blanket licence and retains its own rights record. ## Version control

The parent project is a local Git repository with Git LFS support available, but no files are currently tracked through LFS and no tracking rules have been chosen. LFS may later support large public, distributable assets when actual storage and transfer needs justify a rule; it is not a rights, confidentiality, privacy, or publication boundary. Private or restricted material must remain outside the public repository and its history whether or not LFS is available.
