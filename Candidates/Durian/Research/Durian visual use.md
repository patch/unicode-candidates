## Evidence categories

Durian imagery appears in several contexts that should remain analytically separate:

- **Functional or non-marketing use:** a durian pictogram regulates behaviour, identifies a prohibited item, or otherwise communicates beyond promotion. The repeated prohibition signs in [Durian signage](<Durian signage.md>) are the strongest current evidence of an independent symbol convention.
- **Marketing or brand use:** a durian form identifies a product, producer, retailer, event, or commercial category. This remains relevant, especially when unrelated organizations in different territories converge on a recognizable form.
- **Inline-text integration:** the durian form substitutes for or behaves like a letter, word, or punctuation mark inside otherwise ordinary text. This can demonstrate that designers and readers treat the image as a compact graphic unit, although a one-off stylistic treatment does not by itself establish a general convention.

Marketing evidence should not be used as if it were functional signage, but it should not be excluded. A future corpus should compare frequency, independence, territory, venue, semantic role, and visual convergence across both categories.

## Proposal relevance

Ducoff and Forbidden do **not** independently demonstrate an encoding gap. Each can be interpreted as a stylistic rendition of an existing encoded character—LATIN CAPITAL LETTER O (`U+004F`) and FULL STOP (`U+002E`) respectively—and can be represented as ordinary text without a durian character.

Their value is supplementary: two different brand identities integrate simplified durian forms into type design and rely on the form remaining legible at approximately character scale. Alongside functional signage, they help document the durian’s reduction to a compact, recognizable graphic unit. They should support visual-conventionalization and recognizability arguments in a proposal, not be counted as independent proof that plain text cannot express the underlying content. Ducoff remains a documented design project rather than verified sustained commercial deployment.

These examples show that the search direction is viable, not that pictorial letter substitution is widespread. The next stage should look for independent deployed brands rather than accumulate speculative portfolio concepts.

## Collection protocol

For each marketing or inline-text example, record:

- territory and local subdivision, using the project’s identifier rules;
- organization, brand, designer, and whether the work was actually deployed;
- date range, media, venue, and target market;
- exact word or phrase and `Language Name (language_code)`;
- the graphic’s role: standalone icon, letter substitution, punctuation, rebus, product illustration, seal, or background decoration;
- whether the source explicitly identifies the shape as durian or the identification is a project inference;
- independence from previously documented examples;
- source provenance and publication rights.

Search trademark offices, packaging-award archives, design-studio case studies, retail photographs, company websites, and relevant stock libraries. Search for replacements of round letters—especially LATIN CAPITAL LETTER O (`U+004F`) and LATIN SMALL LETTER O (`U+006F`)—as well as spiky substitutions for other letters, punctuation-like terminal marks, and rebus constructions. Treat stock libraries as both a discovery source and, where useful, a separately licensable source; apply [Evidence storage and publication](<../../../Project/Evidence storage and publication.md>) and [Content sources and licensing](<../../../Project/Content sources and licensing.md>) before acquisition.

## Evidence-strength rule

One commissioned logo proves only that a designer found a durian form legible in that composition. Independent use by unrelated organizations, across multiple territories and contexts, is stronger evidence of a shared visual convention. Even repeated letter or punctuation substitutions normally remain stylistic variants of the underlying encoded characters. Functional signs remain a distinct and generally stronger evidential category because the pictogram must communicate a rule rather than merely evoke a product.
