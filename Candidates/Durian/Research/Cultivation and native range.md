## Native range

There are two defensible answers to “where durians grow natively”.

### Any accepted *Durio* species

Kew Plants of the World Online accepts 29 *Durio* species and gives the genus a native range from Indo-China to western Malesia, with native distribution entries for Borneo, Malaya, Myanmar, Sumatra, and Thailand ([R01](Sources.md#r01)). World Flora Online provides an independent current taxonomic cross-check for the genus ([R04](Sources.md#r04)). Singapore is added from the Singapore National Parks Board’s native record for *Durio singaporensis* ([R03](Sources.md#r03)). Mapped to present territories identified by ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes, the genus-level native set is:

| Country code | Territory | Botanical basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| BN | Brunei | Borneo | High |
| ID | Indonesia | Borneo and Sumatra | High |
| MY | Malaysia | Borneo and Malaya | High |
| MM | Myanmar | Explicit Kew distribution | High |
| SG | Singapore | NParks native record for *D. singaporensis* | High |
| TH | Thailand | Explicit Kew distribution | High |

Kew’s “Malaya” is a botanical region rather than a present state. Singapore is not inferred from that label alone; it has direct national-flora evidence.

### The common cultivated durian, *Durio zibethinus*

Kew gives the native range as “Sumatera to Borneo” and records Malaya, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, Vietnam, India, the Philippines, Java, and other islands as introduced ([R02](Sources.md#r02)). Mapped to present territories:

| Country code | Territory | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| BN | Brunei | Part of Borneo | High |
| ID | Indonesia | Sumatra and Indonesian Borneo | High |
| MY | Malaysia | Malaysian Borneo | High |

This narrower set is the more relevant answer when “durian” means the principal traded fruit. The broader genus set is relevant to biological origin, wild diversity, and conservation.

## Market-oriented cultivation and active commercial projects

The tables provide a tiered answer rather than a false binary. Seventeen territories have documented market production: eleven industrial or statistically established cases and six niche commercial cases. Two further territories have active projects expressly intended to develop commercial production, but recurring market output has not been demonstrated to the same standard. Inclusion does not imply equal scale or current-year statistical coverage.

### Industrial or statistically established

| Country code | Evidence of cultivation | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| BN | The Department of Agriculture and Agrifood publishes durian production in national agricultural statistics ([C07](Sources.md#c07)). | Established national crop; small international scale |
| CN | Hainan reported a third commercial harvest in 2025, with Sanya fruit reaching the market and an expanding industry chain ([C12](Sources.md#c12)). | Emerging domestic industry at commercial scale |
| ID | Statistics Indonesia reports large national production; 2023 preliminary production was about 1.044 million tonnes in the agricultural food-consumption compendium ([C03](Sources.md#c03), [N02](Sources.md#n02)). | Major producer |
| KH | The 2023 Cambodia Agriculture Survey records durian holdings and area; a 2025 government report gave 5,289 ha planted and 36,656 tonnes annual output ([C08](Sources.md#c08)). | Established and expanding |
| LA | Lao reporting describes approximately 170 farms, about 20,000 ha and commercial farm sales, though definitions and maturity of plantings need reconciliation ([C09](Sources.md#c09)). | Established but statistics need normalization |
| LK | Sri Lanka’s Department of Agriculture maintains a crop guide and FAO reported 6,000 tonnes in 2008 ([C11](Sources.md#c11), [C06](Sources.md#c06)). | Established minor producer |
| MY | The 2024 Agriculture Census reports 793,683.2 tonnes for 2023 with state detail ([C02](Sources.md#c02)). | Major producer |
| MM | Government sources document growers, markets, production zones in Mon, Kayin and Tanintharyi, and a market still mainly domestic ([C10](Sources.md#c10)). | Established regional industry |
| PH | The Philippine Statistics Authority publishes national production, area, and bearing-tree series; Davao Region produced 64,621.28 tonnes in 2024 ([C04](Sources.md#c04)). | Established industry concentrated in Mindanao |
| TH | The Office of Agricultural Economics publishes annual area, production, and yield; official reporting put the 2026 crop on course for 2.07 million tonnes ([C01](Sources.md#c01)). | Largest established export industry |
| VN | The national statistical office reported 863,500 tonnes in 2023 and 1.8209 million tonnes in 2025 ([C05](Sources.md#c05)). | Major, rapidly expanded producer |

FAO’s 2008 compilation is a useful historical cross-check rather than a current league table: Thailand 800,000 tonnes, Indonesia 602,700, Malaysia 376,300, Vietnam 85,000, Philippines 51,400, and Sri Lanka 6,000 ([C06](Sources.md#c06)).

### Verified niche commercial cultivation

| Country code | Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| AU | The Northern Territory government states that commercial growers operate in northern Queensland and around Darwin ([C13](Sources.md#c13)). | Niche commercial |
| IN | Kerala plantation sources document fruit farms and cultivation, while an official commissioned sector study calls national production and market size insignificant ([C14](Sources.md#c14)). | Niche commercial, concentrated in the humid south |
| PR | University of Hawai‘i extension lists Puerto Rico among commercial cultivation locations; a Puerto Rican farm reports limited but increasing wholesale quantities after Hurricane Maria ([C16](Sources.md#c16)). | Niche commercial; hurricane-disrupted |
| TW | Taiwan’s Agriculture and Food Agency reported a 1.2 ha farm with about 250 trees, increasingly stable production, and online orders in 2020 ([C22](Sources.md#c22)). | Niche commercial; near the climatic limit |
| TZ | Reports from Zanzibar document producing trees, spice farms, roadside piles, and fruit sold locally ([C17](Sources.md#c17)). | Smallholder market production; medium confidence |
| US | USDA Hawai‘i statistics recorded 35 harvested trees, 2,800 lb of production, and US$9,200 sales in 2016 ([C15](Sources.md#c15)). | Very small but directly verified commercial crop |

United States (US) and Puerto Rico (PR) have separate ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes and therefore separate primary records.

### Emerging commercial project

| Country code | Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| PG | A 2024 project at Wawin was expressly framed around commercial/export planting; the national agriculture plan also called for expanded household and market production ([C18](Sources.md#c18)). | Commercial intent verified; recurring output not yet demonstrated |
| VE | The Turimiquire Foundation reports producing trees and a project sponsoring cultivation and sale to establish durian as a Venezuelan cash crop ([C23](Sources.md#c23)). | Producing commercialization project; independent/official verification still desirable |

## Leads not counted as verified commercial production

| Country code | Lead | Why it remains unverified |
|---|---|---|
| BR | A Brazilian nursery says trees occur occasionally in home and commercial orchards in Bahia and Pará ([C20](Sources.md#c20)). | Seller-authored claim; no official output, farm sale, or agricultural census record found |
| CR | Caribbean Costa Rican farms advertise producing trees and commercial potential ([C19](Sources.md#c19)). | Individual farm and property marketing; continuity and actual sales are unclear |
| MG | Reviews repeat that cultivation extends to Madagascar ([C21](Sources.md#c21)). | No current primary agricultural or commercial-production source located |
| PF | Australia maintains a phytosanitary export pathway for fresh durian from French Polynesia ([C24](Sources.md#c24)). | Market access does not establish that commercial shipments or current producing farms exist |

Fiji’s agriculture policy says durian is worth exploring for tourism/export niches, but does not establish current commercial output ([C25](Sources.md#c25)). Bangladesh and Uganda appeared in weak secondary claims during screening but were not retained as country records without a traceable agricultural source.

## Interpretation cautions

- National output may include backyard or mixed-orchard fruit; this still demonstrates an established crop but not necessarily a specialized industry.
- Planted area and bearing area are not interchangeable.
- A cultivar trial, nursery listing, or surviving tree is not evidence of commercial production.
- Current political borders are not botanical regions. The mapping to ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 territories should remain explicit.
- Wild *Durio* diversity is not equivalent to edible-fruit supply; NParks notes that *D. singaporensis* seeds lack the familiar edible aril ([R03](Sources.md#r03)).
